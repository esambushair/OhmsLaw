📚 Lab Manual & Step-by-Step Guide

  
This virtual physics lab models all eight syllabus experiments with interactive wiring, optical benches, and observation tables:

  

   

    1. OHM'S LAW
    
V–I Graph, Screw Gauge & ρ

    
Wire the circuit, plot V vs. I, measure wire diameter d and length L, and compute specific resistance ρ = R·A / L.

   

   

    2. METRE BRIDGE - 1
    
Wheatstone Balance Method

    
Place Known Resistance R in the left gap and Unknown Wire X in the right gap to find null point l: X = R(100−l)/l.

   

   

    3. CONVEX LENS
    
u–v & 1/u–1/v Graph Method

    
Measure rough focal length with a distant object, record 6 sharp (u, v) pairs, and determine f from both graphs.

   

   

    4. CONVEX MIRROR
    
Using Auxiliary Convex Lens

    
Focus wire gauze onto screen, insert convex mirror to retrace rays onto gauze, measure R, and find f = R / 2.

   

   

    5. REFRACTIVE INDEX
    
Liquid Lens & Plane Mirror

    
Remove needle parallax without liquid (f_g) and with liquid drops (f) to find μ = 2 − f_g / f.

   

   

    6. FIGURE OF MERIT
    
Half-Deflection Method

    
Find galvanometer resistance G = RS/(R−S) by reducing deflection from θ to θ/2, and compute k = E/(R+G)θ.

   

   

    7. CONCAVE LENS
    
Lenses Out of Contact

    
Focus real image I_1 with convex lens L_1, insert concave lens L_2, shift screen to I_2, and find f = uv/(u−v).

   

   

    8. METRE BRIDGE - 2
    
Series Combination of Coils

    
Measure R_1 and R_2 separately, connect r_1 + r_2 in series in the right gap, measure R_3, and verify R_3 = R_1 + R_2.

   

  

 


 

  

   
Metre Bridge - 2 (Experiment No. 8 — Series Combination)

   
Aim: To verify the laws of combination of resistances in series using a metre bridge.

   
X = R · (100 − l) / l   |   Series Law: R3 (Experimental) = R1 + R2 (Theoretical)

   
    
    
  1. Step A (Coil r1): Connect coil r_1 in the right gap and known resistance box R in the left gap. Find null points for 4 values of R (e.g., 2, 4, 6, 8 Ω) to compute Mean R_1.
    2. 
    
  2. Step B (Coil r2): Replace r_1 with coil r_2 in the right gap and take 4 readings to compute Mean R_2.
    3. 
    
  3. Step C (Series r1 + r2): Connect both coils r_1 and r_2 in series across the right gap, take 4 readings to compute Mean R_3, and compare R_3 with R_1 + R_2.
    4. 
   

  


  

   
Concave Lens (Experiment No. 7 — Lenses Out of Contact)

   
Aim: To determine the focal length of a concave lens using a convex lens.

   
1/f = 1/v − 1/u   ⇒   f = (u · v) / (u − v)  (negative focal length)

   
    
    
  1. Step 1 (Real Image I1): Mount convex lens L_1 at a fixed distance from the illuminated wire gauze O and slide the screen to obtain a sharp inverted image at I_1.
    2. 
    
  2. Step 2 (Insert Concave Lens L2): Place concave lens L_2 between convex lens L_1 and screen I_1 without disturbing O or L_1. Measure distance L_2I_1 = u (cm).
    3. 
    
  3. Refocus at I2: Slide the screen alone backward until a clear image I_2 is obtained, and measure L_2I_2 = v (cm). Calculate f = uv / (u − v) across 6 positions of L_2.
    4. 
   

  


  

   
Figure of Merit of a Galvanometer (Experiment No. 6)

   
Aim: To determine the resistance of a galvanometer by half-deflection method and to find its figure of merit.

   
G = (R · S) / (R − S)   |   k = E / ((R + G) · θ)

   
    
    
  1. Step 1 (Full Deflection θ): Keep Key K_2 OPEN and close Key K_1. Adjust high resistance box R (0–10,000 Ω) to get a large, even deflection θ within the 30-div scale (e.g., 30, 28, 26, 24 div).
    2. 
    
  2. Step 2 (Half Deflection θ/2): Without changing R, close Key K_2 and adjust shunt resistance box S until the galvanometer needle drops to exact half-deflection θ/2.
    3. 
    
  3. Calculate G & k: Record 4 sets and compute G = RS / (R − S) and figure of merit k = E / ((R + G)θ) in A/div.
    4. 
   

  


  

   
Refractive Index of Liquid (Experiment No. 5)

   
Aim: To find the refractive index of water using a convex lens & a plane mirror.

   
μ = 2 − (f_g / f)   where f_g = (x_1 + x_2)/2 (glass lens), f = (x_1 + x_2)/2 (combination)

   
    
    
  1. Part (a) — Without Liquid (fg): Place the plane mirror horizontally on the iron stand base and place the convex lens on it. Slide the horizontal needle vertically until its tip coincides with its inverted image without parallax. Measure x_1 (from upper lens surface) and x_2 (from plane mirror surface); then f_g = (x_1 + x_2)/2.
    2. 
    
  2. Part (b) — With Liquid (f): Put a few drops of water on the plane mirror and place the convex lens over it (forming a plano-concave liquid lens). Remove parallax again and record x_1 and x_2 to find f = (x_1 + x_2)/2.
    3. 
    
  3. Calculate μ: Substitute into μ = 2 − f_g / f (for water, μ ≈ 1.33–1.34).
    4. 
   

  


  

   
Convex Mirror (Experiment No. 2)

   
Aim: To find the focal length of a convex mirror using a convex lens.

   
f = R / 2   (where R = distance between convex mirror and screen)

   
    
    
  1. Step 1 (Focus Screen): Mount the convex lens L in front of illuminated wire gauze O and slide the screen to obtain a sharp inverted image at C.
    2. 
    
  2. Step 2 (Insert Convex Mirror): Place the convex mirror M between lens L and the screen with its reflecting face towards the lens. Slide the mirror until rays strike normally and retrace their path to form a sharp inverted image side by side with the wire gauze O.
    3. 
    
  3. Measure R & f: Measure the distance R = x_screen − x_mirror between the mirror and the screen; then f = R / 2. Repeat 5 times by changing object distance u_L.
    4. 
   

  


  

   
Convex Lens (Experiment No. 1)

   
Objective: To find the focal length of a convex lens by plotting graphs between u & v and between 1/u & 1/v.

   
1/f = 1/v − 1/u   ⇒   f = (u · v) / (u + v)  (using magnitudes u, v > 0)

   
    
    
  1. Distant Object Method: Mount the convex lens on the holder and focus a distant tree onto the white screen. Measure the screen distance to get rough focal length f.
    2. 
    
  2. Optical Bench (6 Readings): Keep the illuminated wire gauze in front of the lens—take 3 points between f and 2f and 3 points beyond 2f—and adjust the screen until the inverted cross-wire image is sharp.
    3. 
    
  3. From u–v Graph: Draw the 45° bisector (u = v) to meet the hyperbola at (2f, 2f); then f = (OA + OB) / 4.
    4. 
    
  4. From 1/u – 1/v Graph: Extrapolate the straight line to cut both axes at 1/f; then f = 2 / (x-intercept + y-intercept).
    5. 
   

  


  

   
Metre Bridge - 1 (Wheatstone's Principle)

   
Aim: To find the resistance of a given wire using a metre bridge.

   
X / R = (100 − l) / l   ⇒   X = R · (100 − l) / l

   
    
    
  1. Wiring: Connect Leclanché cell E and one-way key K across ends A and C. Connect Resistance Box R in the left gap (L1–L2) and Unknown Wire X in the right gap (R1–R2). Connect Galvanometer G between central terminal B and Jockey D.
    2. 
    
  2. Opposite Deflection Check: Unplug 2 Ω in Resistance Box R, close Key K, and touch Jockey D first at End A (0 cm) and then at End C (100 cm). The galvanometer needle must deflect in opposite directions.
    3. 
    
  3. Null Point (30–70 cm): Slide Jockey D gently until Galvanometer G reads 0. Note balancing length AB = l (cm) and BC = 100 − l (cm).
    4. 
    
  4. 4+ Observations: Repeat for R = 2 Ω, 3 Ω, 4 Ω, 5 Ω, 6 Ω and calculate Mean X.
    5. 
   

  


  

   
Reading the Analog Voltmeter & Ammeter

   
Analog scales have major numbered marks and smaller unnumbered ticks between them. Always check the Least Count (LC) shown at the top-right of each dial:

   
LC = Value of 1 Major Division ÷ Number of Subdivisions

   
    
    
  • Voltmeter 0–3 V: Major marks every 0.5 V with 10 subdivisions → LC = 0.05 V per small tick.
    • 
    
  • Voltmeter 0–10 V: Major marks every 2 V with 10 subdivisions → LC = 0.2 V per small tick.
    • 
    
  • Ammeter 0–100 mA: Major marks every 20 mA with 20 subdivisions → LC = 1 mA per small tick.
    •

Mastering the Screw Gauge (Micrometer)

  
The screw gauge measures very thin wires to 0.01 mm precision (LC = 1 mm / 100 = 0.01 mm).

  

   

    
Step A: Find Zero Error (Before Inserting Wire)

    
    
     
  1. Remove the wire and slide the thimble left until "Ratchet clicking" appears.
    2. 
     
  2. Positive Zero Error (CSR ≤ 50): Zero Error = +(CSR × 0.01) mm
    3. 
     
  3. Negative Zero Error (CSR > 50): Zero Error = −((100 − CSR) × 0.01) mm
    4. 
    

   

   

    
Step B: Measure the Wire Diameter

    
Observed = MSR + (CSR × 0.01) mm
Corrected d = Observed − (Zero Error)
